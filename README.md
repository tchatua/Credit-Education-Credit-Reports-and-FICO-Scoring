# How to Lower Your Bills and Raise Your FICO Score

Lowering monthly costs can make it easier to pay debts on time, which is one of the most important ways to build a healthy credit history. There is no instant fix, and a particular action cannot guarantee a higher score. FICO scores are based on information in your credit reports, and results vary by person and scoring model.

## Find ways to lower your bills

1. **Review a few months of spending.** List recurring bills, subscriptions, due dates, interest rates, and fees. Cancel services you do not use and check for duplicate or unexpected charges.
2. **Ask providers about lower-cost options.** Contact your phone, internet, insurance, and other service providers to ask about current promotions, cheaper plans, discounts, or a due-date change. Compare the total cost, including fees and contract terms, before switching.
3. **Shop around for insurance.** Compare equivalent coverage from several insurers. A lower premium is not a good deal if it leaves you without coverage you need.
4. **Reduce utility use where practical.** Review your utility provider's efficiency programs and consider low-cost changes such as adjusting the thermostat or reducing standby power.
5. **Contact creditors before missing a payment.** If a payment is becoming unaffordable, ask the lender or service provider about hardship options or a payment plan. Get any changed terms in writing and understand fees, interest, and possible credit-reporting effects.
6. **Put savings to work.** Once you have a small emergency cushion, consider using some freed-up cash to pay down high-interest debt while keeping all required payments current.

## Habits that can support a stronger FICO score

- **Pay every credit account on time.** Set reminders or automatic minimum payments if you can keep enough money in the account. If you miss a payment, bring the account current as soon as possible and contact the lender.
- **Keep credit-card balances low relative to their limits.** Paying balances down can reduce your reported utilization. Issuers may report balances at different times, so paying before the statement closes may help keep the reported balance lower. You do not need to carry a balance or pay interest to build credit.
- **Check your credit reports for errors.** In the United States, use [AnnualCreditReport.com](https://www.annualcreditreport.com/) to access reports from the nationwide credit bureaus. If you find inaccurate information, dispute it with the bureau and, when appropriate, the company that supplied it. Correcting an error does not guarantee a score increase.
- **Apply for credit selectively.** Several applications in a short period can lead to hard inquiries and may affect scores. Compare offers and apply only when the account fits your needs.
- **Think carefully before closing older cards.** Closing an account can affect available credit and utilization. Consider fees, spending habits, and account terms before deciding.
- **Be cautious about credit-repair promises.** Accurate negative information generally cannot simply be removed, and no company can guarantee a score increase. You can dispute inaccurate information yourself without paying a credit-repair company.

## What to expect

Payment history and amounts owed are major factors in many FICO scoring models; other factors include the age and mix of accounts and new credit activity. The exact formula and impact vary across FICO versions and lenders. Many household bills do not ordinarily appear on credit reports unless they are reported to a bureau or become part of a collection or other reportable event. Check your reports and the terms of your accounts to understand what is being reported.

Steady on-time payments and lower debt balances can help over time, but changes may take a while to appear and no particular score or timeline is guaranteed. This guide is for general education, not individualized financial or legal advice.
